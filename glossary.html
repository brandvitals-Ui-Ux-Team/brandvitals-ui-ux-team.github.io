Glossary

The marketing terms we use, defined.

Every term below appears somewhere on this site. Each definition stands on its own, so you can link to it or quote it.

This glossary defines the marketing terms Brand Vitals uses with clients in Egypt and Saudi Arabia: measurement such as ROAS and CPA, tracking such as GA4 and Conversions API, paid media, websites and search, brand, and the market terms that shape plans in both countries.

Last updated 16 October 2026

Money and measurement

ROAS (return on ad spend)

ROAS is revenue from ads divided by what the ads cost. A ROAS of 3 means every 1 spent brought back 3 in sales. ROAS says nothing about profit on its own, so read it next to your margin.

Break-even ROAS

Break-even ROAS is one divided by your gross margin. With a 40% margin it is 2.5: below that, ads lose money on every sale. Vitals Forecast shows it first for this reason.

CPA (cost per acquisition)

CPA is what you pay in ads for one result you care about: a purchase, a qualified lead or a booking. It is the number to set against margin or deal value.

CPM (cost per thousand impressions)

CPM is the price of showing an ad a thousand times. It rises with competition, for example in busy seasons, and it is the starting point of every cost-per-result estimate.

CTR (click-through rate)

CTR is the share of people who saw an ad and clicked it. It mostly measures the creative and the offer, not whether the click turned into a sale.

Conversion rate

Conversion rate is the share of visitors or clicks that complete the action you want, such as a purchase or a form. Speed, trust and the offer move it more than design alone.

AOV (average order value)

AOV is revenue divided by the number of orders. Bundles, delivery thresholds and upsells raise it, which lets you afford a higher cost per order.

Contribution margin

Contribution margin is what is left from a sale after product, delivery, payment and return costs. Targets set on it stay honest when prices and costs move.

LTV (customer lifetime value)

LTV is the total margin a customer brings over all their orders. Brands with repeat buyers can pay more for the first order because the later ones carry the profit.

CAC (customer acquisition cost)

CAC is everything you spend to win one new customer: media, creative and tools. Comparing CAC with LTV tells you whether growth pays for itself.

Attribution window

The attribution window is how many days after a click or view a platform still credits a sale to an ad. Compare platforms only when the windows match.

Blended results

Blended results divide total revenue or leads in your store or CRM by total ad spend across all platforms. They correct for each platform claiming the same sale.

Tracking

Pixel

A pixel is a piece of code from Meta, TikTok, Snap or Google that runs in the browser and reports page views and conversions back to the ad platform.

Conversions API

Conversions API sends conversion events from your server straight to the ad platform. It keeps working when browsers block the pixel, so the algorithm learns from real outcomes.

Server-side tracking

Server-side tracking routes measurement through a server you control instead of many browser scripts. Data is more complete, pages load faster and you decide what is shared.

GA4 (Google Analytics 4)

GA4 is Google's analytics tool. It counts events, not sessions, so it is only as good as the events you define: purchases, leads, WhatsApp clicks and calls.

Google Tag Manager

Google Tag Manager is a container for tracking tags. Marketing can add or change tags without a code release, inside rules the developers agree to.

Consent mode

Consent mode adjusts Google tags to each visitor's cookie choice. Tracking respects the choice while modelled data fills some of the gap.

Offline conversions

Offline conversions send outcomes that happen outside the website, such as a closed deal in the CRM or a paid cash-on-delivery order, back to the ad platforms.

Paid media and creative

Prospecting

Prospecting campaigns reach people who have not engaged with the brand yet. They feed every later stage, so cutting them first starves retargeting a few weeks later.

Retargeting

Retargeting shows ads to people who visited, watched or added to cart. Its ROAS often looks high because many of those people would have bought anyway.

Performance Max

Performance Max is a Google Ads campaign type that buys across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display and Maps from one budget. It needs clean conversion data and brand exclusions to stay efficient.

Negative keywords

Negative keywords stop search ads from showing for searches you do not want, such as jobs, free or a competitor's support line. Reviewing them weekly protects the budget.

Click-to-WhatsApp ads

Click-to-WhatsApp ads open a chat with the business instead of a website. They suit markets where customers want to ask before buying, as long as replies are fast and tracked.

Creative testing

Creative testing runs several hooks, angles and formats against the same audience and budget rules, so the winner is chosen by results instead of taste.

Hook rate

Hook rate is the share of viewers still watching a video after the first three seconds. It shows whether the opening earns attention before the message starts.

Creative fatigue

Creative fatigue is when the same audience has seen an ad too often and results drop. A steady supply of new variations is the cure, not a bigger budget.

UGC (user-generated content)

UGC-style ads are filmed the way customers and creators film themselves. They feel native to the feed and are cheap to produce in many variations for testing.

Websites and search

CRO (conversion rate optimization)

CRO is the practice of finding where visitors drop off and testing changes that help more of them buy or enquire, so the same traffic brings more customers.

A/B test

An A/B test splits visitors between two versions of a page and compares results. It needs enough conversions per version before the difference can be trusted.

Core Web Vitals

Core Web Vitals are Google's measures of loading speed, responsiveness and layout stability on real devices. Slow pages waste ad spend before anyone reads the offer.

hreflang

hreflang tags tell search engines which page is the Arabic version and which is the English one, so each searcher lands on the right language.

Technical SEO

Technical SEO makes a site easy to crawl, index and understand: clean URLs, speed, structured data, sitemaps and no duplicate pages competing with each other.

Local SEO

Local SEO helps a business appear in map and near-me searches through a verified Google Business Profile, consistent name, address and phone, and genuine reviews.

AI search optimization (GEO)

AI search optimization prepares content to be found, understood and quoted by answer engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews: clear answers, defined terms and consistent facts.

Landing page

A landing page is built for one campaign and one action. It repeats the ad's promise, answers the main objections and removes every link that does not lead to the action.

Brand

Brand positioning

Positioning is the one idea a brand wants to own in the customer's mind, against named alternatives. Every message, visual and offer should be checked against it.

Brand guidelines

Brand guidelines set how the logo, colours, type, tone of voice and imagery are used, in Arabic and English, so every team and agency produces work that looks like one brand.

Egypt and Saudi Arabia

Cash on delivery (COD)

Cash on delivery means the customer pays the courier when the order arrives. It is common in Egypt, and it means a placed order is not yet revenue until it is delivered and paid.

VAT (value added tax)

VAT is added where the law requires it: 14% in Egypt and 15% in Saudi Arabia. Saudi customers expect prices shown with VAT included.

Saudization (Nitaqat)

Saudization is the Saudi programme that sets the share of Saudi nationals companies must employ by sector and size. Hiring plans in the Kingdom are built around it.
