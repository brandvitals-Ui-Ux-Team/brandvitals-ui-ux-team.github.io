Less is more: focus your catalog to lift ROAS

Why narrowing paid media down to a few hero products can multiply your return on ad spend, and how to choose them.

Advertising a whole catalogue on a modest budget spreads data thin. Pick three to five hero products scored on contribution margin, organic demand, repeat purchase and how easy they are to show in a short video, give each its own creative, landing page and budget, and keep a smaller catalogue campaign for retargeting.

Last updated 16 October 2026

Many stores advertise their whole catalog and let the algorithm figure it out. With a large catalog and a modest budget, the result is thin data spread across hundreds of products, and no single product gets enough attention to win.

What a hero product is

A hero product is one that is easy to understand in a few seconds, has healthy margins, already sells without ads, and can bring customers who later buy other things. It does not need to be your best-selling item overall.

How to pick three to five

Pull the last six months of sales and score each product on four things.

  • Contribution margin after shipping and returns.
  • Organic demand: sales without paid traffic.
  • Repeat purchase rate of customers who bought it first.
  • How easy it is to show in a short video.

Then build the account around them

Give each hero product its own creative, landing page and budget. Keep a smaller catalog campaign running for retargeting so the rest of the range still sells. Review every quarter, because heroes change with seasons and trends.

A simple scoring sheet

List the products with at least some sales in the last six months and give each a score from one to five on the four criteria: contribution margin, organic demand, repeat purchase of first-time buyers, and how easy it is to show. Add the scores and look at the top ten, then use judgment: drop products with stock problems or long delivery times, and keep the three to five that tell the clearest story.

What each hero product gets

  • Its own creative set: several hooks and formats, refreshed before they tire.
  • Its own landing page: the promise from the ad, answers to the main objections, delivery and payment options.
  • Its own budget line, so results are readable product by product.
  • A bundle or cross-sell that introduces the rest of the range after the first order.

Keep the rest of the range selling

Focusing the ads does not mean hiding the catalogue. A smaller catalogue campaign retargets visitors with the products they viewed, email and WhatsApp flows suggest related items after purchase, and the site navigation still shows the full range. The hero products bring people in; the rest of the range raises order value and repeat purchases.

When to change your heroes

Review the heroes every quarter and before each major season. Swap a product out when its contribution margin falls, its creative keeps tiring faster than new ideas can replace it, or stock becomes unreliable. In Saudi Arabia, seasonal moments such as Ramadan and White Friday may call for a different set; in Egypt, moving prices can change which products still carry a healthy margin.

Landing pages for hero products

A hero product deserves more than its standard product page. Build a landing page that repeats the ad's promise, shows the product in use, answers the main objections such as size, delivery time and returns, shows payment options, and ends with one clear action. In Egypt, add a WhatsApp button for questions; in Saudi Arabia, show prices in SAR with VAT included.

Budget per hero, not per platform

Plan the budget around the products first: how much each hero can spend profitably given its margin and target cost per order, then split that across platforms. This keeps the conversation on which products deserve more money, and stops a platform from absorbing budget for products that do not pay back.

Signs the focus is working

  • Cost per purchase on hero products falls or holds while spend grows.
  • New customers who start with a hero go on to buy other products.
  • Sales of the rest of the range hold through the catalogue campaign and retention flows.
  • Contribution margin after marketing improves across the store, not only on the heroes.

Telling the team why the ads focus

Focusing the ads can worry people who own other product lines, because their products disappear from the feed. Share the scoring sheet, explain that the heroes bring new customers who then buy across the range, and show the catalogue campaign and retention flows that keep every line selling. Review the heroes together each quarter, so the choice is open and data-led rather than a matter of taste.

When a product line has a strong case, such as a new launch or a seasonal moment, give it a time-limited test as a hero rather than spreading the budget again.

The takeaway

Focus creates data, and data creates winners. Fewer products in your ads often means more revenue from your whole store.

Key terms

Terms used in this article.

AOV (average order value)
AOV is revenue divided by the number of orders. Bundles, delivery thresholds and upsells raise it, which lets you afford a higher cost per order.
Contribution margin
Contribution margin is what is left from a sale after product, delivery, payment and return costs. Targets set on it stay honest when prices and costs move.
Landing page
A landing page is built for one campaign and one action. It repeats the ad's promise, answers the main objections and removes every link that does not lead to the action.
Retargeting
Retargeting shows ads to people who visited, watched or added to cart. Its ROAS often looks high because many of those people would have bought anyway.
