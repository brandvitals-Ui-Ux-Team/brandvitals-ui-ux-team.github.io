The KPI stack every e-commerce CEO should watch weekly

ROAS is not enough. The five numbers that actually predict whether your e-commerce growth will hold.

An e-commerce CEO needs five weekly numbers, not platform ROAS: blended MER, new-customer acquisition cost, contribution margin after ads, shipping and returns, conversion rate by device, and 90-day repeat purchase rate. In cash-on-delivery markets, measure delivered revenue, not placed orders.

Last updated 16 October 2026

A good ROAS can hide a business that is losing money, and a weak ROAS can belong to one that is growing well. CEOs need a short list of numbers that connect marketing to profit, reviewed every week.

The five numbers

Keep this on one page and review it in the same meeting every week.

  • Blended MER: total revenue divided by total marketing spend.
  • New customer CAC: marketing spend divided by first-time buyers.
  • Contribution margin after ads, shipping and returns.
  • Conversion rate by device, especially mobile.
  • Repeat purchase rate within 90 days.

Why platform ROAS is not on the list

Each platform takes credit for sales it touched, so the totals add up to more than you actually sold. It is useful for optimizing inside the platform, not for deciding how much the business should spend.

Local realities

In Egypt and much of the region, cash on delivery and returns can change the picture completely. Measure delivered revenue, not placed orders, and include failed deliveries in your CAC.

How to calculate each number

  • Blended MER: total revenue for the week divided by total marketing spend across every platform and agency fee.
  • New-customer CAC: marketing spend divided by the number of first-time buyers in the same period.
  • Contribution margin after marketing: revenue minus product cost, shipping, payment fees, returns and ad spend.
  • Conversion rate by device: orders divided by sessions, split into mobile and desktop.
  • 90-day repeat rate: the share of first-time buyers who order again within 90 days.

A one-page weekly report

Put the five numbers on one page with this week, last week and the same week last month, and add one line on what changed and one decision. Keep the same order every week so the meeting compares like with like. Platform dashboards stay useful for the team that runs the ads, but the CEO meeting runs on this page.

Cash on delivery changes the maths

In Egypt many orders are paid in cash at the door, and some are refused or never confirmed. Count revenue when it is delivered and paid, include failed deliveries in acquisition cost, and track confirmation rate by campaign, because some audiences place more orders they never accept. Sending delivered orders back to the ad platforms teaches them to find buyers who pay.

When the numbers disagree

If platform ROAS rises while blended MER falls, platforms are claiming sales that would have happened anyway, often through retargeting. If new-customer CAC rises while total revenue holds, growth is relying on repeat buyers. Each pattern points to a different fix, which is why the stack is read together rather than one number at a time.

Setting targets for each number

Targets come from the business, not from benchmarks. Contribution margin after marketing should stay positive at the level the business plan needs. New-customer CAC should stay below what a new customer is worth over time, which depends on margin and repeat purchase. MER should stay above the level where total marketing pays for itself. Write the targets next to the numbers so the weekly meeting reads them against something.

Where the data comes from

Revenue, orders, returns and repeat purchases come from the store or order system, not from ad platforms. Marketing spend comes from each platform's billing plus agency and production fees. Sessions and conversion rate by device come from analytics such as GA4. Pulling each number from its source of truth avoids the double counting that makes platform reports add up to more than you sold.

Mobile conversion deserves its own line

Most visitors from paid social arrive on a phone. When mobile conversion rate falls while desktop holds, the problem is usually on the site: speed, a checkout step, a payment option or a form that is hard to complete on a small screen. Splitting conversion rate by device turns a vague drop in sales into a specific page to fix.

Running the weekly meeting

Keep the weekly review short and fixed: the same five numbers, in the same order, against the same targets. Spend the first minutes on what moved and why, then agree one or two decisions with an owner and a date. Save deeper analysis for a monthly session, so the weekly meeting stays about decisions rather than debating data sources.

Over time the team learns which numbers move first when something goes wrong, and the meeting becomes an early warning system rather than a report.

The takeaway

If you only watch one number, watch contribution margin after marketing. It is the one that pays the bills.

Key terms

Terms used in this article.

Blended results
Blended results divide total revenue or leads in your store or CRM by total ad spend across all platforms. They correct for each platform claiming the same sale.
Contribution margin
Contribution margin is what is left from a sale after product, delivery, payment and return costs. Targets set on it stay honest when prices and costs move.
CAC (customer acquisition cost)
CAC is everything you spend to win one new customer: media, creative and tools. Comparing CAC with LTV tells you whether growth pays for itself.
LTV (customer lifetime value)
LTV is the total margin a customer brings over all their orders. Brands with repeat buyers can pay more for the first order because the later ones carry the profit.
