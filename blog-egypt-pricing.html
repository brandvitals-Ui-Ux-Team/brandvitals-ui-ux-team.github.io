Marketing in Egypt when prices keep moving

When costs and prices change every few months, last quarter's targets stop making sense. How to keep media efficient and customers buying.

When prices move every few months in Egypt, marketing targets have to move with them. Set cost per order from contribution margin, review it monthly, sell value instead of discounts, and keep your best always-on campaigns running so demand and retargeting pools survive uncertain months.

Last updated 16 October 2026

In Egypt, a cost per order that looked healthy six months ago can be a loss today. The answer is not to stop advertising, but to measure in terms that move with prices.

Measure margin, not only ROAS

Set a target cost per order as a share of contribution margin, and update it every time prices or costs change. A fixed ROAS target quietly turns profitable campaigns into losing ones.

Sell value, not discounts

  • Lead with what lasts, saves or protects: durability, warranty, value per use.
  • Offer installments and smaller pack sizes before cutting price.
  • Show the price clearly; hiding it costs you more messages than sales.

Keep the always-on base

Brands that go dark during uncertain months lose search demand and retargeting pools that take months to rebuild. Trim spend, but keep your best campaigns running.

How to calculate a target cost per order

Start from one order. Take the average selling price, then subtract product cost, packaging, delivery, payment fees and the cost of returns and failed cash-on-delivery orders. What is left is contribution margin per order. Decide how much of that margin you are willing to spend to win the order, and that share is your target cost per order.

Repeat the calculation whenever a price list, a supplier cost or a courier rate changes. The number on the dashboard then means the same thing as the number in the bank account, even when both are moving.

Why a fixed ROAS target fails when prices move

ROAS divides revenue by ad spend. If your costs rise and your prices rise by less, the same ROAS now leaves a thinner margin, and a campaign that used to pay can quietly start losing money. Break-even ROAS, one divided by gross margin, rises at the same time. Watching ROAS alone hides that shift; watching margin shows it the same month.

Selling value without a price war

Discounts train customers to wait and shrink the margin you are trying to protect. Value messages work harder: what the product saves, how long it lasts, what is included, how fast it arrives and how easy it is to return. Bundles and delivery thresholds raise the average order value without cutting the price of a single item.

When a price increase is unavoidable, say so plainly and explain what stays the same. Customers in a moving market respect brands that are honest about prices more than brands that hide them until checkout.

A monthly review in four questions

  • Did contribution margin per order change this month, and why?
  • Is the target cost per order still a safe share of that margin?
  • Which campaigns stayed above break-even, and which fell below it?
  • Is the always-on base still running, so search demand and retargeting pools are kept warm?

Price communication on ads and landing pages

When prices change often, old ads become a liability: a customer clicks an ad with last month's price and meets a higher one at checkout. Keep prices out of evergreen creative, or tie them to a clear validity date, and update landing pages the same day the price list changes. Where a price must appear, show what is included, such as delivery or installation, so the comparison is fair.

Installments and payment options

Customers in Egypt pay by cash on delivery, InstaPay, cards and installments. When prices rise, installments and clear payment options can protect conversion better than a discount, because they lower the barrier without lowering the margin. Show the payment options on the product page and in WhatsApp replies, not only at the last step of checkout.

Planning budgets in a moving market

Fix the target as a share of margin, not the budget as a fixed amount. When margin per order falls, the affordable cost per order falls with it, and the budget that can be spent profitably shrinks; when margin recovers, the budget can grow again. Agreeing this rule in advance avoids emergency cuts that switch off the campaigns you will need when demand returns.

What to tell your team when prices change

Price changes affect every team that talks to customers. Share the new price list, the date it applies from and the reason in one short note, and update the WhatsApp replies, the website and the ads on the same day. Agree how to handle customers who saw an older price, such as honouring it for orders already placed. Consistent answers protect trust more than any single discount.

Then watch the effect for two to four weeks: conversion rate, average order value and the questions customers ask. If conversion falls, look first at how the price is explained and paid, before reaching for a discount.

The takeaway

Re-price your targets, not just your products. Review cost per order against margin every month.

Key terms

Terms used in this article.

Break-even ROAS
Break-even ROAS is one divided by your gross margin. With a 40% margin it is 2.5: below that, ads lose money on every sale. Vitals Forecast shows it first for this reason.
Contribution margin
Contribution margin is what is left from a sale after product, delivery, payment and return costs. Targets set on it stay honest when prices and costs move.
AOV (average order value)
AOV is revenue divided by the number of orders. Bundles, delivery thresholds and upsells raise it, which lets you afford a higher cost per order.
