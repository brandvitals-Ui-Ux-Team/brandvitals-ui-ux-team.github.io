Saudi Arabia's calendar is your media plan

Ramadan, Founding Day, National Day, White Friday and payday: how to plan spend and creative around the moments Saudi customers actually buy.

In Saudi Arabia demand moves with a few moments: Ramadan and Eid, Founding Day, National Day, White Friday and payday around the 27th. Plan each one in three waves, awareness before, conversion during and retargeting after, brief creative about six weeks ahead, and let the budget follow the calendar.

Last updated 16 October 2026

In Saudi Arabia, a flat monthly budget leaves money on the table. Demand moves with a handful of moments every year, and the brands that win plan their creative and spend weeks before them, not the day they start.

The moments that move demand

  • Ramadan and Eid: the biggest month for food, fashion, gifting and real estate. Viewing peaks late at night.
  • Founding Day (22 February) and National Day (23 September): pride-led creative performs, generic discounts do not.
  • White Friday in November: the main discount event for e-commerce in the Kingdom.
  • Payday around the 27th: many categories see a lift in the days after salaries land.

Plan in three waves

Build awareness two to three weeks before the moment, push conversion during it, and retarget the people who engaged right after. Each wave needs its own creative, not the same ad with a new budget.

Creative that feels local

Saudi audiences notice when an ad was written for somewhere else. Use Saudi dialect where it fits the brand, local faces and settings, and prices in SAR with VAT included.

Build the year on one page

Put every moment that matters to your category on a single calendar: the dates, the six-week creative brief deadline, the start of each wave and the offer you plan to run. Add payday weeks to every month. When the calendar is visible to marketing, sales and stock planning, nobody discovers a season the week it starts.

What each wave needs

  • Awareness, two to three weeks before: video and creative that introduce the offer or the story, aimed at new audiences, judged on reach among the right people and cost per engaged viewer.
  • Conversion, during the moment: direct offers, prices in SAR with VAT included, and landing pages ready for the traffic, judged on cost per purchase or qualified lead.
  • Retargeting, right after: reminders for people who engaged but did not buy, and follow-up for new customers, judged on cost per purchase and repeat orders.

Ramadan is different every night

During Ramadan viewing peaks late at night, so schedule delivery and customer service for the hours people are actually on their phones. Creative that respects the month, such as family, generosity and gathering, fits better than hard selling. Eid then works as its own short moment with gifting and fashion demand.

Founding Day and National Day

Founding Day on 22 February and National Day on 23 September reward pride-led creative more than generic discounts. Use local faces, settings and dialect where they fit the brand, and keep offers simple. These days also crowd the feed, so the creative brief needs to start early enough for production and approvals.

Stock, delivery and service around the peaks

A season plan is only as good as what happens after the click. Check stock levels for the products you will push, confirm delivery capacity and timelines for the peak weeks, and plan customer service hours for the times your customers are active, including late nights in Ramadan. A campaign that sells out early or promises delivery it cannot keep spends money creating disappointed customers.

Offers that fit the moment

Each moment calls for a different offer. White Friday is a discount event, so the offer needs to be clear and comparable. Founding Day and National Day reward pride-led creative more than generic discounts. Ramadan suits bundles, gifting and family-sized options. Payday weeks suit simple reminders of products people already considered. Prices in every case appear in SAR with VAT included.

Reviewing a season after it ends

Within two weeks of each moment, review it while the details are fresh: what each wave cost per result, which creative worked, where stock or delivery limited sales, and what the retargeting wave brought back. Write the lessons into next year's calendar entry, so the plan improves every year instead of starting again from memory.

Budgeting across the year

Start from the calendar, not from twelve equal months. Mark the weeks that matter for your category, give them a larger share of the year's budget, and keep an always-on base running between them so audiences and learning do not reset. Remember that costs rise in busy seasons because more brands compete for the same attention, so the plan should judge each season on cost per result, not on spend alone.

Keep a small reserve for opportunities you did not plan, such as a product that sells faster than expected during a season, and decide in advance who can approve moving it.

The takeaway

Put the year's moments in a calendar now and brief creative six weeks ahead of each one. The budget follows the calendar, not the month.

Key terms

Terms used in this article.

CPM (cost per thousand impressions)
CPM is the price of showing an ad a thousand times. It rises with competition, for example in busy seasons, and it is the starting point of every cost-per-result estimate.
Retargeting
Retargeting shows ads to people who visited, watched or added to cart. Its ROAS often looks high because many of those people would have bought anyway.
VAT (value added tax)
VAT is added where the law requires it: 14% in Egypt and 15% in Saudi Arabia. Saudi customers expect prices shown with VAT included.
Creative fatigue
Creative fatigue is when the same audience has seen an ad too often and results drop. A steady supply of new variations is the cure, not a bigger budget.
